A focused polish pass

Retain: the warm board, cream background, system typography, SF Symbols, native grouped Form, navigation, confirmation prompts, and share sheet. These already give the game a quiet identity.
  1. Fix accessibility-size gameplay first. Replace overlapping placement with normal vertical layout. Let status rows stack and supporting content scroll while preserving usable board space.
  2. Consolidate match setup. One native New Game sheet should contain opponent, difficulty, stones, and board size, followed by an explicit Start action. Confirm resets consistently during active play. Rename PvP to “2 Players”; use “Restart” during play and “Play Again” after results.
  3. Make Settings conditional. Keep the native Form. Show custom-size fields only for Custom and difficulty only for AI. Resolve duplicate custom/preset selection tags.
  4. Simplify iPad composition. Use one status region, board, and familiar action dock with sensible maximum widths. Remove repeated inspector information and expose New Game during active play.
  5. Clarify supporting flows. Emphasize Play Again, quiet the About contact card, improve replay targets/context, and strengthen history and share metadata.

These recommendations follow Apple’s guidance on restrained materials, a clear hierarchy of buttons, and adaptable layouts. Board-specific composition recommendations are design judgments based on the captured app.

Every captured state

Step 01 · Open gameplay

Board and everyday play

Strong foundation

The warm board and restrained palette communicate the game immediately.

Polish: Reduce repeated match configuration in the HUD. Keep turn, player, and time information compact; give the board the strongest visual priority and leave setup to New Game.

Step 02 · Open Settings

Native settings structure

Refine the choices

The grouped Form feels familiar and straightforward.

Polish: Hide irrelevant AI rows in 2 Players mode instead of leaving disabled controls visible. Consolidate match configuration into New Game and keep Settings focused on ongoing preferences.

Step 03 · Open About

A quieter information sheet

Simplify the surface

The content is approachable, but the contact card adds unnecessary material treatment. Green from the underlying Haptics toggle bleeds through.

Polish: Use ordinary contact rows, toolbar Done, and a sheet that accommodates larger text. Keep glass restrained.

Step 04 · Open empty History

An actionable empty state

Clear starting point

The empty history presentation is uncluttered.

Polish: Retain this clear empty state. Source review identifies a need to distinguish an empty archive from filters matching no games; the latter state was not visually verified.

Step 05 · Choose New Game

One complete setup flow

Consolidate

The menu is native, but setup is spread across this menu, the HUD, and Settings.

Polish: Replace the scattered choices with one native sheet: opponent, difficulty when relevant, stones, board size, and Start. Use “2 Players” for immediate comprehension.

Step 06 · Play against AI

Keep attention on the turn

Good gameplay baseline

The board remains the central feature during AI play.

Polish: Give player/turn status a stable hierarchy. Keep difficulty as compact context, and place changes in the New Game sheet so setup controls do not compete with play.

Step 07 · Increase text to largest size

Repair gameplay reflow

Highest priority

Gameplay information overlaps and truncates at the largest Dynamic Type size.

Polish: Use normal vertical layout, expandable rows, and adaptive stacking. Allow supporting content to scroll when needed. Apple explicitly recommends layout changes for larger text.

Step 08 · Inspect Settings at largest text

A useful native reference

Reflows correctly

The Form adapts to larger text successfully in this capture.

Polish: Preserve this system-managed behavior when simplifying settings. Use the same willingness to grow rows and wrap content in gameplay, rather than forcing the default-size arrangement.

Step 09 · Choose board size

Unambiguous selection

Needs source correction

The native picker is an appropriate lightweight control.

Polish: Source review finds duplicate custom/preset tags. Give each choice a unique identity; reveal editable dimensions only for Custom. Move match-specific size selection into the New Game sheet.

Step 10 · Request a reset

Preserve the native prompt

Works clearly

The system confirmation is familiar and legible.

Polish: Apply the same protection to every action that replaces an active board, including setup changes. Broader reset-path consistency is a source-review recommendation; this capture verifies the native prompt. Keep “Restart” wording during play.

Step 11 · Show placement guidance

Keep coaching outside play

Move the tip

The precision tip explains useful interaction, but obscures top intersections.

Polish: Place brief guidance outside the board and let it dismiss cleanly. Dense-board touch and loupe precision need a separate physical-device check before choosing further interaction changes.

Step 12 · Complete a winning game

Make the next game obvious

Refine action hierarchy

The visible winning line gives the result useful context.

Polish: Preserve that line. Make Play Again the prominent action and Share secondary; avoid equal emphasis for every option. Follow Apple’s button hierarchy guidance.

Step 13 · Inspect saved History

Readable match summaries

Keep the thumbnails

The board thumbnail helps identify a saved game.

Polish: Establish a consistent row hierarchy: result/opponent first, then date, size, and move count. Only one simulated completed match was available; large archives and exact filter/search edge cases remain unchecked.

Step 14 · Replay the saved game

Clear playback and move context

Improve controls

Replay stepping and playback were exercised successfully.

Polish: Give separate playback actions 44-point hit regions. Show the selected move number, player, and coordinate together so viewers can connect the control state with the board.

Step 15 · Play on portrait iPad

A single composed game surface

Simplify iPad hierarchy

The larger canvas offers useful room, but repeated information weakens focus.

Polish: Use one status region, board, and the same action dock as iPhone. Apply sensible width caps and add a dedicated New Game action during active play.

Step 16 · Switch to Dark Mode

Legible player indicators

Small clarity improvement

The dark palette retains the game’s restrained character.

Polish: Add a subtle outline to the black stone indicator. Use monospaced digits for clocks and named accessibility labels that identify both player and elapsed time. Contrast ratios were not measured.

Step 17 · Share a result

Useful shared-item metadata

Keep the system sheet

The share sheet is native; the captured item preview appears placeholder-like.

Polish: Supply a result thumbnail and clear title. Validate the preview on a physical device and release build before treating its icon appearance as a confirmed product defect.

Step 18 · Rotate iPad to landscape

Use width without duplication

Refine composition

Landscape provides space for a comfortable board and nearby controls.

Polish: Remove the duplicate inspector. Place one concise status/action region beside the board where useful, preserving the same game structure. Check narrow windows as part of adaptive layout work.

Step 19 · Open iPad Settings

Preserve familiar controls

Solid native base

The system settings presentation fits the platform.

Polish: Apply the same conditional fields and consolidated setup as iPhone. Keep the form comfortably readable at a sensible width; verify it when the iPad window becomes narrow and text size increases.

Coverage and limits

This review uses the current simulator captures and supporting source inspection. One simulated match was completed; replay stepping/playback and board accessibility actions were exercised. The review did not cover a full VoiceOver user journey, physical-device behavior, measured contrast, iOS 18 fallback, draws, large history datasets, windowed iPad layouts, or dense-board touch/loupe precision. Exact filter/search edge cases were not visually checked. Recommendations distinguish visible issues from source findings and follow-up validation.

Design recommendations only. App source was not changed for this report.
